# Fix length, parity and taps before rules

Terra Imperium already copies the right things from its two models: its battle economy runs at about Age of Empires IV pace, its wall rule matches Civilization VI's (melee does 15% to walls), its stacks behave like Civ VII's packed Commander armies, and its per-nation ages avoid the forced resets that cost Civ VII half its Steam reviews. The rule changes that matter most are therefore not new systems. They are four corrections: make auto-resolve break units at the same loss share as the real-time battle (today Auto breaks a fresh unit at about 27% losses and the RTS at about 67%), cut how long one battle and one game take (an economy assault may run 30 minutes, as long as a whole AoE IV match, and a Normal game is 923 turns against Civ VI's 500), let most cities and the battle economy run themselves on a phone (Civ VII Towns, an economy steward), and replace the uneven age-gap ratio (1.24x to 1.8x per age today) with a fixed per-age step before the Information and Future ages are added. Behind those come a Civ VII-style early Dominance end, Civ VI's double war weariness abroad, self-embarking, phone control groups and a radial menu, and a small set of decision points for the empty mid-game. Things the owner has ruled out stay out: no forced rout for the player, no captives (so no AoE monk conversion), no armour-subtraction rewrite that would break auto-resolve parity, and no Civ VII-style identity reset at age changes. Each recommendation below says whether its outside rule rests on a cited source or on an unverified number, and every number we propose for our own code is a starting value to test with `balance-sim` or `battle-lab`, not a measured result.

## How to read the evidence marks

Every external rule below carries one of two marks. **Verified** means the research notes found it in a cited source (official pages, patch notes, Civilopedia, or a wiki or community page quoted with its URL). **Unverified** means the number came from memory, a disputed forum figure, or a single player's measurement; we use it only as a direction, never as a target. Our own rules are quoted from the code at commit 15e06f83 on `claude/integration`, with the file path. Proposed numbers for our code are design guesses and are marked "to tune". The battle AI math (target scoring, per-engagement withdraw, kiting, influence maps) and the macro AI's expected-value and utility choices are covered in `plans/research-ai-math.md`, which the owner parked on 2026-10-20; this report points to its rows instead of repeating them.

## The real-time battle: AoE's rules against ours

### Battle economy runs at AoE IV pace on top of an army AoE never has

**AoE rule.** In AoE2 DE a villager gathers wood at 0.39 per second, gold 0.379, stone 0.36 and farm food 0.32, carrying 10 per trip, and costs 50 food and 25 seconds at the Town Center (**verified**: [AoE wiki, Villager](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Villager_(Age_of_Empires_II)); [aoe2.ai](https://aoe2.ai/en/eco)). AoE IV roughly doubles that to about 0.75 per second for wood, gold and stone (**verified**: [AoE wiki, Resource AoE4](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Resource_(Age_of_Empires_IV))). Population caps at 200, raised by houses of 5 (AoE2) or 10 (AoE4) (**verified**: [AoE wiki, Population](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Population); [Gamewatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/age-of-empires-4-population-limit)). AoE2 has an idle-villager button and on Xbox a D-pad shortcut for it (**verified**: [ageofempires.com, Xbox controls](https://www.ageofempires.com/learn-to-play/controlling-your-empire-gathering-resources-xbox/)). AoE2 farms run dry and are reseeded for 60 wood; whether AoE4 farms are infinite is **unverified**.

**Our rule.** Every campaign land battle except raids, sacks and naval fights runs a full base economy: 5 starting workers, four resources gathered at food 0.65, wood 0.6, stone 0.5 and gold 0.4 per worker-second with a carry of 10, farms renewable at x0.7, mines x1.25, 300 population a side, a worker for 50 food in 12 seconds, and trained companies of 200 strength that demobilise after the battle (`src/battle/data/economy.js:32-174`, `src/battle/sim/economy.js:39-40`). The HUD already has an "Idle n" worker button (`src/components/battle/BattleHud.jsx:489`) and one-tap worker presets.

**Gap.** Our rates sit at AoE IV speed, which is right for short fights, and renewable farms remove AoE2's reseed chore, which is right for a phone. The real difference is structural: an AoE player builds the whole army from the economy, while our player arrives with a campaign army and the economy only adds 200-strength auxiliaries. On a 844x390 screen the player must therefore run two games at once (army micro and base micro) for a payoff that is a fraction of the army they brought.

**Change.** Keep the rates and the renewable farm. Add an economy steward: a per-battle toggle on the battle card ("Run my base for me") that hands the player's economy to the existing economy AI at a chosen level, so the player commands only the army. **Why:** AoE Mobile shows the opposite extreme (fully automatic battles) is called bland by reviewers (**verified**: [Gaming.net](https://www.gaming.net/reviews/age-of-empires-mobile-review-ios-android/)), while AoE's console version keeps the full simulation and compresses input instead (**verified**: [ageofempires.com, Xbox controls](https://www.ageofempires.com/learn-to-play/match-goals-advanced-controls-xbox/)); a steward keeps the player in charge of what matters without forcing either extreme. **How:** `economyAI.js` already plans workers, drop-offs, production buildings and towers from `AI_ECONOMY` per difficulty (`src/battle/sim/economyAI.js:19-31`). Add a `setup.stewards[side]` level to the setup built by `buildCommandedSetup`, and in the economy step run `economyAI` for that side while the player's army orders still come from the order log. Because the steward's decisions are a pure function of sim state, the replay in `battleVerify.js` reproduces them without any new order type; bump `SETUP_VERSION` so old checkpoints are refused. Default the steward to "on" for phones (layout `phone-landscape`) and "off" elsewhere, and let a player's own economy order on a worker take that worker back from the steward. Priority: high.

### Strength-scaled damage is the right core, but the counter and age multipliers are blunt

**AoE rule.** AoE2 subtracts the matching armour (melee or pierce) from each attack component and floors damage at 1; AoE IV uses `max(1, (base + upgrades) x multiplier + (bonus + upgrades) x bonus multiplier - armour)`, with percentage resistances for siege (**verified**: [AoE wiki, Attack](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Attack); [AoE wiki, Armor AoE4](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Armor_(Age_of_Empires_IV))). Counters come from large class bonuses, such as a pikeman's +22 against cavalry, and from armour, such as the skirmisher's pierce armour (**verified**: [AoE wiki, Pikeman](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Pikeman_(Age_of_Empires_II))). Blacksmith chains add attack and armour by age (**verified**: [aoe2.guide](https://aoe2.guide/the-blacksmith/)). Civ VI and VII instead convert a strength difference into damage exponentially: Civ VII's developer-given formula is `30 x e^(0.040 x diff) x random(0.7 to 1.3)` (**verified** as a wiki extract of the developer formula: [Civ7 Combat wiki](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Combat_(Civ7))), and Civ VI's community fit `30 x e^(diff/25)` means +17 strength doubles damage and +30 is a one-hit kill (**verified** as a community fit: [CivFanatics, Hans Lemurson](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/hans-lemurson-figures-out-the-combat-formula.606147/); [CivFanatics, Valuing Units](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/valuing-units-buildings.646107/latest)).

**Our rule.** A hit deals `round(strength x perHitFraction x mult x variance)` with `BASE_DAMAGE_RATE = 0.1` over `EXCHANGE_SECONDS = 6`, so a squad in contact deals about 1.67% of its own strength a second before multipliers; the multiplier stacks a uniform counter (x1.75 for the favoured class, x0.6 for the unfavoured), an age ratio, terrain, promotions, generals, flank x1.3, rear x1.5, charge x1.25, elevation within plus or minus 10%, and plus or minus 10% seeded variance (`src/battle/sim/combat.js:31-158`, `src/engine/battle.js:48-89`, `src/data/unitClasses.js:20-106`). There is no armour stat. The age multiplier is the ratio of the two ages' infantry `baseAttack`: Stone 7, Bronze 10, Classical 18, Kingdoms 26, Gunpowder 34, Modern 42 (`src/data/unitClasses.js:200-205`, `src/data/ages.js:26-75`).

**Gap.** Strength-scaled damage is the correct choice for regiments that carry campaign strength, and the same `computeHitMultiplier` drives auto-resolve, which is what keeps Command and Auto honest. Switching to AoE's armour subtraction would break that link and the parity calibration, so we should not copy it. The weak spot is the age ratio. A one-age gap is worth 1.43x (Bronze over Stone), 1.8x (Classical over Bronze), 1.44x, 1.31x and 1.24x (Modern over Gunpowder): the value of being one age ahead swings by a factor of 1.45 depending on which ages meet, and two new ages (Information, Future) need `baseAttack` numbers invented for them. Civ's lesson is that one fixed conversion from "points ahead" to damage makes bonuses readable and balanceable.

**Change.** Replace the ratio of `baseAttack` with a fixed per-age-step factor read from a small integer table: gap 0 = 1.00, 1 = 1.40, 2 = 1.96, 3 or more = 2.74 (to tune; 1.40 is near the median of today's one-step values). **Why:** it gives every age pair the same edge, needs no invented stats for Information and Future, and mirrors Civ's "flat points, exponential effect" model (verified curve; our step value unverified and to be set by parity runs). **How:** in `src/data/ages.js` add `AGE_GAP_MULT_Q8 = [256, 358, 502, 702]` and a helper `ageGapMult(attackerAge, defenderAge)` that indexes it by `agesFrom` distance (negative gaps use the reciprocal entry); call it from the side base multiplier in `src/engine/battle.js` where the `baseAttack` ratio is used today, so the battle sim and auto-resolve change together. Keep `baseAttack` only for display if anything reads it. Re-run the battle-lab parity on 32 seeds (the eight-ages plan's R19 bar) and the `lanchester.calibration.test.js` fit. Priority: high, because it must land before the eight-ages work gives new ages their stats.

A second, smaller change: let a unit class pair override the uniform x1.75 and x0.6 per age through an optional `counterOverrides` field in `unitClasses.js` (for example Kingdoms pikemen against knights, Modern ATGM teams against tanks). AoE's identity comes from such specific matchups (verified for pikeman and camel bonuses above). Pair it with giving each people's signature unit one rule tag from a short menu (+25% against one class, +1 tile range, charge, or +10% speed), since `src/data/signatureUnits.js:1-16` says they fight with the base unit's rules today, while AoE civs differ in real rules such as the Inca's 10-pop houses and Persian double-HP Town Centers (**verified**: [AoE wiki, House](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/House_(Age_of_Empires_II)); [aoecoach, Town Center](https://aoecoach.com/en/resources/buildings/town-center)). Both feed through `computeHitMultiplier`, so parity holds. Priority: low, content-heavy.

Do not add in-battle blacksmith upgrades or an in-battle age-up. AoE's age-ups cost minutes of play (AoE2 Feudal 130 seconds, Castle 160, Imperial 190: **verified**, [AoE wiki, Feudal Age](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Feudal_Age_(Age_of_Empires_II))), and our campaign already supplies the age and its promotions, which is the whole point of linking the layers.

### Morale: keep the soft penalty, but make Auto break units at the same point

**AoE rule.** Neither AoE2 nor AoE IV has morale or routing: soldiers fight until killed and retreat only on the player's order (**verified**, though the best source is a blog comment thread and a fan proposal for morale on the official forum: [ACOUP](https://acoup.blog/2022/07/01/collections-total-generalship-commanding-pre-modern-armies-part-iiic-morale-and-cohesion/comment-page-1/); [AoE forum, Morale System](https://forums.ageofempires.com/t/morale-system/215105)).

**Our rule.** Morale falls `round(100 x damage / startStrength x 1.2)`, so a fresh squad reaches the rout line of 20 at about 67% losses (`src/battle/sim/moraleMath.js`). The AI's squads rout; the player's never do in a commanded battle (`canRout` false), becoming Shaken instead (deal x0.6, take x1.3) and staying fully controllable (`src/battle/sim/morale.js:29-40`, `src/battle/setup/buildBattleSetup.js:621-624`). Auto-resolve, however, uses `MORALE_LOSS_PER_PERCENT = 3`, which breaks a fresh unit at about 27% losses (`src/engine/battle.js:56-57`).

**Gap.** The player rule is already met and matches AoE's spirit: nobody takes the player's units away. The problem is a hidden mismatch inside our own game. Total War's loudest complaint is auto-resolve disagreeing with the real battle in both directions (**verified** as player reports: [Steam WH3](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1142710/discussions/2/591759013796937462); [Steam WH2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/594570/discussions/0/1480982971175660014)). With Auto routing units at 27% losses and the RTS at 67%, Auto battles end sooner with more units "fled" and fewer "fallen" than the same fight commanded, even if the parity harness tunes the win rate to match.

**Change.** Set auto-resolve's morale loss per percent so a fresh unit breaks at the same loss share as the RTS, or, if the round-based model needs a faster break to end in 8 rounds, scale per round rather than per percent so the break point in losses is equal. **Why:** the battle you skip should produce the same ledger shape as the battle you fight. **How:** derive both constants from one exported number, `BREAK_LOSS_SHARE` (0.67 today in the sim), in `src/battle/sim/moraleMath.js`, and compute `MORALE_LOSS_PER_PERCENT = (100 - MORALE_ROUT_THRESHOLD) / (100 x BREAK_LOSS_SHARE)` in `src/engine/battle.js`. Then rerun the parity harness and extend it to compare the fallen and fled shares from `battleLedger.js`, not just the winner. If the owner prefers Auto to stay bloodier on purpose, write that down as a rule rather than leave it as two unrelated constants. Priority: highest; it is a two-constant change that protects trust.

Two related, smaller items. The AI's own retreat decision is a fixed share (0.15 to 0.30 by difficulty) rather than a per-fight forecast; that is row 4 of `plans/research-ai-math.md` (Lanchester withdraw with hysteresis, AI only), parked by the owner. And the Shaken state should be visible on the squad card on a phone (an icon on the squad pick circle), because a soft penalty only works as a soft signal if the player can see it.

### Siege: our wall rule already equals Civ VI's, so add work for the attacker, not HP

**AoE and Civ rules.** AoE2 castles have 4,800 HP and cost 650 stone, Town Centers 2,400 HP, and garrisoned units add arrows (**verified**: [Liquipedia, Castle](https://liquipedia.net/ageofempires/Castle); [aoecoach, Town Center](https://aoecoach.com/en/resources/buildings/town-center)). AoE IV rams attack only buildings, take +20% from melee, deal +300 against walls, and infantry can build rams and springalds in the field (**verified**: [AoE wiki, Battering Ram AoE4](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Battering_Ram_(Age_of_Empires_IV)); [Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3757716456)). AoE2 monks convert units by a timed roll (**verified**: [AoE wiki, Conversion](https://ageofempires.fandom.com/wiki/Conversion)). Civ VI walls take 15% from melee, 50% from ranged and 100% from siege (**verified**: [Civilopedia, City Combat](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/combat_9/)). Total War: Warhammer III's siege rework made attackers build crewed ladders and gave defenders a supplies currency for barricades and towers, after critics called sieges "the same fight" every time (**verified**: [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/total-war-warhammer-3s-siege-rework-is-a-big-step-towards-perfection/)).

**Our rule.** Keep HP `(1500 + 750 x fortLevel) x 1.5 if capital`; towers fire by HP left and crew; non-siege blows on structures x0.15; siege x3 against fortification and x0.5 in the field; a walled keep takes x0.6 while the ring stands, and x2.5 storm damage once a wall or gate is down; garrisons fire at x0.8 and are thrown out under 25% HP (`src/battle/setup/buildBattleSetup.js:84-104`, `src/battle/sim/combat.js:35-39, 170-196`, `src/battle/sim/siegeRules.js`). Real cities load their manifest (houses, walls, gates by material and age) and carry damage between battles (`src/battle/setup/cityBattle.js`). On the macro map a siege grinds wall HP but never takes a city (`src/engine/sieges.js`).

**Gap.** Our x0.15 for non-siege blows is exactly Civ VI's melee rule, and our keep HP sits in AoE's range, so the numbers are fine. Two differences remain. Ranged units also get x0.15 where Civ VI gives them 50%; that is a defensible choice (AoE buildings also shrug off arrows, though their pierce armour values are **unverified**). The bigger gap is playtest 4's "least fun" moment: a 30-minute assault that bled out under towers because squads stood idle (`plans/playtest-4-audit.md:273`), with the idle-squad cause fixed since (commit 13133406). An army that arrives without siege has no in-battle path to a breach except training at a Siege Workshop (120 wood, 60 gold, 40 seconds, `src/battle/data/economy.js:161-174`).

**Change.** Add AoE IV's field engineering: any infantry squad of the attacker can build a ram (Bronze onward) or ladders (Stone, matching the existing Ladder Crews) on the spot from its side's stock, with no workshop. **Why:** it turns "bleed under towers" into a choice the player makes in ten seconds, and it is the attacker-side work Total War added to make sieges differ. **How:** add one `fieldBuild` order to `src/battle/sim/orderSchema.js` (a new row there, as the replay rule requires), priced in `economy.js` at about 1.5x the workshop price and 30 seconds of the squad standing still (to tune); the result is a siege squad of `TRAIN_STRENGTH` that demobilises like other auxiliaries, so auto-resolve models it through the same `AUX_TRAINED` path in `src/engine/autoBattle.js`. Do not copy monk conversion: master plan decision 37 says lost units are gone, with no captives, and conversion is a capture. Priority: medium.

### Battles last as long as whole AoE matches, and a campaign needs dozens of them

**AoE and hybrid evidence.** A whole AoE IV match averages about 22.7 minutes in one patch's community figures and about 30 minutes at the lowest levels (**unverified** community averages: [AoE forum](https://forums.ageofempires.com/t/standardize-games-to-30-minutes/218364?page=2); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1466860/discussions/0/4357790501742499114/)). Rise of Nations' campaign attacks could run up to 90 minutes each and reviewers listed the time cost as a drawback (**verified**: [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/technology/rise-of-nations-thrones-patriots-expansion/article1136824)). Total War players report auto-resolving every battle by the late game because fights are one-sided (**verified** as player reports: [Steam WH3](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1142710/discussions/0/4416424806096713060)), and Rome II gave siege battles only to province capitals "to avoid the game becoming bogged down in too many sieges" (**verified**: [Gameranx](https://gameranx.com/updates/id/15015/article/total-war-rome-2-provinces-armies-and-generals-details-revealed/)).

**Our rule.** Clocks: no-economy field 5:00 and siege 7:30; economy field 15:00; city assault, landing and real town 30:00; raid 10:00, sack 12:00 (`src/battle/sim/constants.js:7-20`). Small unwalled towns and empty outposts can already fall on Auto without a battle screen (`src/engine/battleThreshold.js`). Command and Auto share inputs, the hit multiplier and one outcome service, and the odds are 200 auto-resolve samples (`src/engine/battleOdds.js`). Playtest 4 found identical Auto battles repeating for 8 turns at one siege (P4-M2).

**Gap.** One economy assault can last longer than an entire AoE IV game, and our phone sessions also carry the empire. Owner decisions 23 and 36 put the economy in every assault and field battle; this report does not overturn them, but the clocks and the threshold are free to tune.

**Change.** Three parts. First, shorten the economy clocks to 10:00 for field and 20:00 for assaults and real towns (to tune with battle-lab's eco bench, checking that a base can still be destroyed inside the clock). Second, offer a "quick field battle" button on the battle card that uses the existing 5:00 no-economy field setup, with the economy version as the default so decision 36 stands. Third, widen `battleThreshold.js` in the Rome II direction: a full RTS assault only for walled cities, capitals or cities above a size (to tune, for example size 6), with smaller places falling on Auto unless the player taps Command. **Why:** fewer, bigger battles keep Command special, and the Total War evidence says players stop fighting manually when every fight is long and one-sided. **How:** the clocks are constants in `src/battle/sim/constants.js` and `buildBattleSetup.js:228`; the quick-field option sets `economy: false` in `buildCommandedSetup` (the replay builds the same setup, so verification holds); the threshold already has a setting. For P4-M2, let the player pick "Auto until the siege ends" once on the defense card in `battleQueue.js`, so repeat assaults on one city resolve without asking each turn. Show the forecast as a casualty band (the 10th to 90th percentile of the 200 samples `battleOdds.js` already draws), not just a win chance, since Total War's preview complaints are about losses, not winners. Priority: high.

## The campaign map: Civ's rules against ours

### Movement already follows Civ; add self-embarking

**Civ rule.** Civ VI charges 1 movement point for open land and 2 or more for hills and woods, makes rivers cost points instead of ending the move, and rounds down so a unit with 1 point left cannot enter a 2-cost tile (**verified**: [CivFanatics, Movement in terrain](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/movement-in-terrain.612700); [Civilopedia, Movement](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/movement_1/)). Land units embark themselves; community testing puts embarking at 2 extra points (**unverified**: [Talk:Movement (Civ6)](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Talk:Movement_(Civ6))). Civ VI's Huge map has 6,996 tiles in total (**verified** as fan-compiled game data: [CivFanatics](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/map-size-and-game-speed.654188)).

**Our rule.** Move points come from km a turn (foot 4, cavalry 8, siege 3 points at f100); open land costs 1, +1 hills, +1 forest, jungle or marsh, +1 desert or tundra, mountains 4 (a pass costs as hills), roads 0.5, railways 0.25; river crossings cost 0.5, 1 or 2 points by river size; unspent points bank up to 4; A* routes, multi-turn marches and Civ-style zone of control (`src/engine/armies.js:9-201`, `src/engine/routes.js`). Land units cross water only when carried by a fleet (`src/engine/fleets.js:3-15`), and playtest 4 found no Embark button (P4-H6, status not checked).

**Gap.** Our movement is Civ VI's with richer rivers and a bank in place of the round-down, which helps slow siege trains cross mountains on a grid with more than four times the land cells of a Civ VI Huge map. The one real gap is water: needing a separate transport fleet is more steps than Civ's self-embark, and on a phone each step is a tap sequence.

**Change.** Let a land stack embark itself from a coastal tile once its nation's age opens the shelf (Classical, the existing `SHELF_OK_FROM`), paying 2 extra points (Civ's tested value, unverified), moving at the fleet pace for its age, and fighting at a heavy penalty if attacked at sea. **Why:** it removes a whole carrier workflow for the common case, while warships stay needed for deep ocean before Gunpowder and for escort. **How:** in `src/engine/fleets.js` create an implicit carrier record when a land stack's route steps onto water (so the existing `embarkedOn` logic, disembark and amphibious assault code all keep working), and add the water step to `findTilePath` costs in `armies.js` with `EMBARK_COST = 2`. The route preview then shows a sea leg like any other, so no new button is needed. Priority: medium.

### Stacks resemble Civ VII's Commander armies, so keep them and show the cap

**Civ rule.** Civ V and VI allow one military unit per tile; Civ VI merges two or three units into Corps (+10 strength) and Armies (+17) late in the game (**verified**: [Civilopedia, Formations](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/combat_12/)). Civ VII keeps one unit per tile in combat but lets a Commander pack units into one moving stack that unpacks beside the target; only Commanders earn XP, and they keep it across Ages (**verified**: [2K, Civ VII Combat dev diary](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/dev-diary/combat/)). Civ VI attackers get +2 strength for each other friendly unit next to the target, and defenders +2 for each friendly neighbour (**verified**: [CivFanatics, Flanking and Support](https://forums.civfanatics.com/resources/civ-vi-flanking-and-support.27610/)). In Civ VII attackers no longer return to their hex after combat (**verified**: [2K dev diary](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-guide/dev-diary/combat/)).

**Our rule.** No one-unit-per-tile rule. A tile holds up to 2x its combat width of one nation's units (12 on plains, 6 in mountains) before each unit loses 10 supply a turn (`src/engine/supplyMeter.js:9-52`); the map draws one flag and one model per tile. A stack attacks an adjacent stack and the whole stack fights one real-time battle; the winner does not advance (`src/engine/fieldBattle.js:1-17`). Co-belligerent allies' and vassals' idle units nearby stand by as reinforcements (`src/engine/battleInputs.js:20-21`).

**Gap.** One-unit-per-tile exists in Civ because each Civ fight is one unit against one unit; ours is a whole-army battle, so stacks are the right model and match where Civ VII ended up. Two Civ ideas fit cheaply: neighbours mattering (flank and support) and the winner following through.

**Change.** First, make the nation's own idle stacks on tiles next to the battle join as reinforcements on the existing timer (`REINFORCEMENT_ARRIVAL_TICKS`, 25 seconds), as allies already do, and list them on the battle card before the fight; Humankind's reinforcement timing was a top complaint because it was hidden (**verified**: [Amplitude beta summary](https://community.amplitude-studios.com/amplitude-studios/humankind/forums/212-closed-beta/threads/41305-summary-of-closed-beta-criticisms-from-steam-reviews?page=1)). Second, after a decisive field win, let the attacking stack step into the vacated tile for free if it has a point left. Third, show the stack cap as "9 of 12" on the stack's flag when over two-thirds full. **Why:** the first is Civ's flank and support in our terms, the second is Civ VII's chained attacks, and the third turns a hidden supply drain into a visible rule. **How:** extend `reinforcementSources` in `battleInputs.js` to include the attacker's and defender's own units within `REINFORCE_RINGS`; in `fieldBattle.js`, on a decisive win, move the stack with the existing tile-move helper (zone of control still applies on later steps); the flag label is a sprite text in `src/components/map/gl/sceneModel.js`. Whether our units heal by territory like Civ VI's 5/10/15/20 HP ladder (**unverified**, forum: [CivFanatics](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/how-much-a-unit-heals-per-turn.519747)) was not checked in the notes. Priority: medium for reinforcements and the cap label, low for the advance.

### Cities: our rules already track Civ VI; the phone needs Civ VII's Towns

**Civ rule.** Civ V charges every city 3 unhappiness plus 1 per citizen and raises tech costs about 5% per city (**verified**: [Carl's Civ 5 Guide](https://carlsguides.com/strategy/civilization5/war/annex-puppet-raze.php); [CivFanatics](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/a-core-set-of-balance-changes.388179/page-12)). Civ VI caps each city with housing and amenities: from population 3, every 2 citizens need one more amenity (**verified**: [Civ Wiki, Amenity](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Amenity_(Civ6))). Civ VII's later settlers found Towns that have no production menu; their production turns into gold, and Firaxis says the change exists because many cities led to "a lot of busywork" (**verified**: [Firaxis Dev Diary 3](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/news/dev-diary-3-managing-your-empire/)). Civ VII also has a soft settlement cap with a happiness hit per settlement over it (the starting cap of 3 and the -5 per settlement are **unverified** guide figures: [Outsider Gaming](https://outsidergaming.com/how-to-raise-settlement-cap-in-civilization-7/)).

**Our rule.** Settlers cost 60 production plus 10 per city owned and need size 2; founding makes an outpost that takes about 8 turns (open land) to become a city, with 1 to 4 outpost slots by age; growth is a logistic curve capped by housing plus 1.75; amenities need `floor((size - 2) / 2)`; one production queue per city with overflow; nine building lines upgraded in place; three adjacency districts; culture-driven borders and gold tile purchases; Civ VI-style loyalty with flips (`src/engine/world/cities.js`, `src/engine/settlers.js`, `src/engine/districts.js`, `src/engine/loyalty.js`). AI cities choose builds with a real utility scorer (`src/engine/aiProduction.js`). Governors already group up to 6 cities for small bonuses (`src/engine/governors.js`).

**Gap.** The amenity rule is Civ VI's, our expansion brakes (settler price per city, outpost slots, loyalty, aggressive expansion and the authority overextension term) already do the job of Civ V's per-city penalty, and Civ VI dropped the per-city tech penalty (that Civ VI has none is **unverified**). What we lack is Civ VII's low-attention city. Playtest 4 counted only 88 production orders in 300 turns and "most of turns 160 to 260 were End Turn" (`plans/playtest-4-audit.md:41-43, 278-300`): players are not micromanaging cities, they are letting queues go stale.

**Change.** Add a per-city "Auto build" toggle that, whenever the queue empties, fills it with the same utility choice the AI uses, under the city's focus (balanced, food, production, gold); default it on for every city except the capital. **Why:** it is Civ VII's Town without a second city class, and it reuses code that already exists and is tested. **How:** store `city.autoBuild` on the city record in `src/engine/world/cities.js`; in the cities phase of `resolveTurn.js`, when the player's city has `autoBuild` and an empty queue, call `aiProduction.js`'s scorer for that city and queue its top pick (seeded tie-breaks keep it deterministic). One toggle in the city sheet and a "set all" in the empire panel cover the phone. Do not add a hard settlement cap; our soft brakes exist already, and the readable fix is one line in the empire panel that lists them together. Priority: high.

### Science and ages: keep per-nation ages, fix the cost ladder, add a fork at each advance

**Civ and AoE rules.** Civ VI's Eurekas pay part of a tech's cost for a map task (the base share of 40% after a patch is **unverified**; the boost system itself is **verified**: [Civilopedia, Dedications](https://www.civilopedia.net/en-US/gathering-storm/concepts/dedications/)). Civ VII resets tech and civics trees at each of its three Ages and forced a civ change at each transition; after a launch at 39% positive Steam reviews (**verified**: [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/less-than-half-of-civilization-vii-s-players-on-steam-are-happy-with-the-game/)), Firaxis added Continuity (units stay and upgrade at the transition), then an optional single-civ mode (**verified**: [1.2.3 notes](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2025-jul-22-patch-1-2-3/); [1.4.0 notes](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2026-may-19-patch-1-4-0/)). AoE IV turns each age-up into a choice between two civ-specific Landmarks with different bonuses (**verified**: [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/age-of-empires-4-landmarks-guide/)). Rise of Nations ran eight ages ending in Information, and Empire Earth fourteen epochs ending in the Nano age (**verified**: [Giant Bomb](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-11993/); [Wikipedia, Empire Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Earth_(video_game))).

**Our rule.** Science fills a current tech with a queue and overflow; 10 techs per age in five lines; a nation advances after 6 of its age's 10; boosts pay 40% once; diffusion discounts techs known nearby (`src/engine/research.js`, `src/data/techTree.js:188`, `src/data/boosts.js`, `src/engine/techDiffusion.js`). The base cost per age is Stone 15, Bronze 17, Classical 82, Kingdoms 1200, Gunpowder 2400, Modern 6000 (`src/engine/research.js:10-11`). The code has six ages; the plan adds Information and Future (`plans/2026-10-20-001-feat-eight-ages-plan.md`). Units fight at their nation's current age through the side multiplier, so an age advance upgrades the army at no cost.

**Gap.** The model already does what Civ VII's players asked for: no forced reset, no identity swap, free unit upgrades (as Continuity now gives). Two things are off. The cost ladder jumps 14.6x from Classical to Kingdoms against 2x to 2.5x elsewhere; the code says it was calibrated to hit target turns for Bronze, Classical and Kingdoms and that Gunpowder and Modern are "first guesses" (`research.js:19-23`), and two more ages need values. And an age advance is a passive event, where AoE IV makes it a decision.

**Change.** First, refit `RESEARCH_AGE_BASE` for all eight ages with `balance-sim` against target turns per age at Normal speed, rather than extending the guessed tail; whether the Kingdoms jump is right is a calibration question, not a bug (**our observation, unverified as a problem**). Second, give each age advance a two-way pick of an "age landmark" bonus (for example Bronze: "+1 food on river farms" or "+10% siege damage"), shown as one decision card. **Why:** the mid-game is where playtest 4 found almost no decisions, and an AoE IV-style fork adds one meaningful choice per age without a new system. **How:** a data table `src/data/ageLandmarks.js` with two entries per age, feeding the existing modifier sheet (`src/engine/modifiers/`); the card fires from the age-advance step in `research.js` and resolves through a new reducer action that the AI answers by seeded pick (the AI may use the top-band pick of research-ai-math row 6 when that work resumes). Use `add-mechanic` before building it. Priority: medium for the fork, high for the cost refit because the eight-ages work depends on it.

### Calendar and pacing: Kingdoms lasts 1.8x Civ's share, and the year freezes

**Civ rule.** Civ VI ties its calendar to the turn number, not to research, through a fixed table per game speed. Standard (500 turns, 4000 BC to 2050 AD) runs 40 years a turn for 75 turns (to 1000 BC), 25 for 60 (to 500 AD), 20 for 25 (to 1000), 10 for 50 (to 1500), 5 for 60 (to 1800), 2 for 50 (to 1900), 1 for 120 (to 2020) and half a year for 60 (to 2050) (**verified**, extracted from the game's GameSpeeds data: [Civilization Wiki, Civ6 GameSpeed values](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Module:Data/Civ6/Base/GameSpeed_Values)). A developer note on the calendar says the values assume the player spends about a tenth of the game's turns in each era (**verified as a modder's quote of patch notes**: [Steam Workshop changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2416644490?p=2)). Civ VII paces by Age instead: about 150 to 200 turns per Age at Standard (**verified as a review figure**: [Game8](https://game8.co/games/Civ-7/archives/495057)).

**Our rule.** The year is only a label tied to research: within the player's age it moves from the age's start year to its end year by the share of the age's 10 techs researched (`src/engine/displayYear.js`; spans in `src/data/ages.js`: Stone 5000 to 2000 BCE, Bronze 2000 to 800 BCE, Classical 800 BCE to 500 CE, Kingdoms 500 to 1500, Gunpowder 1500 to 1900, Modern 1900 to 2300). Game length is a turn limit: Fast 440, Normal 923, Marathon 1514.

**Note (22 Oct).** These measurements ran on code before commit e741e949 ("Research pace and a steady calendar", follow-ups 17 and 18), which landed the same day from the cloud lead session. Rerun the three speeds on current main before using the numbers below; the label freeze may already be fixed.

**Measured.** One full game per speed (balance-sim `worldStats.sim.js`, seed 11, player akkad, scripted policy, checkpoints every 10 turns, so each age boundary is about plus or minus 5 turns; **our measurement**, one seed only). Civ VI's Standard calendar cut into our age spans and stretched to 923 turns gives the comparison column.

| Age (years) | Ours, Normal (turns) | Civ VI shape at 923 turns | Our years per turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone (5000 to 2000 BCE) | about 35 | about 92 | about 86 |
| Bronze (2000 to 800 BCE) | about 20 | about 61 | about 60 |
| Classical (800 BCE to 500 CE) | about 80 | about 96 | about 16 |
| Kingdoms (500 to 1500) | about 250 | about 138 | about 4 |
| Gunpowder (1500 to 1900) | about 210 | about 203 | about 1.9 |
| Modern (1900 to 2300) | about 327 | about 332 | about 1.2 |

Fast showed the same shape (Kingdoms about 130 of 440 turns). Marathon took about 490 turns for Kingdoms before the scripted player was defeated at turn 850. The label also freezes while one expensive tech is researched: on Normal it stood at 500 CE from turn 160 to 210, at 1500 from 410 to 460 and at 1900 from 610 to 660; on Marathon at 500 CE for 240 turns (330 to 570).

**Gap.** Kingdoms takes about 1.8 times Civ's share of the game, which matches the 14.6x research cost jump above and playtest 4's End-Turn-only stretch (turns 160 to 260). Stone and Bronze together take about 55 turns for 4,200 years, against Civ's roughly 150; Stone's length was tuned on purpose in U47 (the share of nations past Stone at the age's target turn), but Bronze is very short. A frozen year reads as a stalled game even when research is moving.

**Change.** First, make Civ's shape the target for the research cost refit (rank 4): turns per age at Normal close to Stone 60 to 90 (keep U47's tuning as the floor), Bronze about 60, Classical about 95, Kingdoms about 140, Gunpowder about 200, and the rest of the game split between Modern, Information and Future when those ages exist. **How:** refit `RESEARCH_AGE_BASE` in `src/engine/research.js` (and the per-age `costMult` of each speed in `src/data/ages.js`) with `balance-sim` until the age-entry turns hit those targets on 4 or more seeds per speed; add `ageEntryTurn_<age>` keys to `worldStats.sim.js` so the check is one number per age. Second, smooth the label: count the current tech's progress share in the interpolation, `year = start + (end - start) x min(1, (done + current progress share) / 10)`, a one-line change in `displayYear.js`; the year stays a label that no rule reads (`ruleYearPurity.test.js` still holds). **Why:** the cost refit removes the mid-game slog where the playtest found nothing to do, and the smooth label stops the calendar from looking stuck. Priority: high for the refit (the eight-ages work needs it anyway), very small effort for the label.

### Economy: gold piles up because our sinks are weaker than Civ VII's

**Civ rule.** Civ VII Towns turn production into gold, and gold buys buildings in Cities (**verified**: [Dev Diary 3](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/news/dev-diary-3-managing-your-empire/)). Rise of Nations caps gathering per resource with a commerce limit raised by research, an explicit anti-snowball rule (**verified**: [Giant Bomb](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-11993/)). Civ unit and building upkeep numbers were not found (**unverified**).

**Our rule.** Gold pays unit upkeep (5 a unit), building upkeep (0.5 x (tier + 1)), forts, advisors, loans and rush buying with a heat surcharge (`src/data/actionCosts.js:135-170`, `src/engine/spending.js`). Playtest 4 ended at turn 300 with 28,521 gold unspent, and rush was the only real sink (`plans/playtest-4-audit.md:138-143`); no AI path buys infrastructure, defences, growth or loans (`plans/economy-use-2026.md`).

**Gap and change.** The owner parked the economy cut list (U34) for a playtest pick, so this report offers inputs to that pick rather than a build order. The Civ VII lesson is that gold should buy finished things: allow buying a whole building tier outright at 2x its production cost in gold, adding the existing heat surcharge (the Civ VI gold-to-production rate of about 2x is **unverified**; to tune). **Why:** it gives the largest pile of unspent currency a direct, visible use and ties into Auto build. **How:** extend `src/engine/spending.js` with a `BUY_BUILDING` action using the same price function as rush, and let `aiEconomy.js` spend on it when its bank exceeds a few turns of upkeep. Priority: medium, after U34.

### Government: give law changes a free moment, as Civ VI does with policy cards

**Civ rule.** Civ VI swaps policy cards in typed slots (2 for Chiefdom, up to 10 late); swapping costs gold except on the turn a civic completes, and returning to a previous government causes at least 3 turns of anarchy (**verified** as wiki extracts: [Civ Wiki, Government](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Government_(Civ6)); [Civ Wiki, Policy card](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Policy_card_(Civ6))). Civ VII ties policy slots to Celebrations from happiness (**verified**: [Dev Diary 3](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/news/dev-diary-3-managing-your-empire/)).

**Our rule.** Five government types with stacking per-age reforms; six law categories with a 5-turn cooldown; a law change costs one decree, government change two, from a bank capped at 3 (`src/data/government.js`, `src/data/laws.js`, `src/data/decrees.js:43-58`). The economy-use study found many laws and reforms never used by player or AI.

**Gap and change.** Our laws are Civ VI's cards with a stricter price, and unused options suggest the price, not the content, is the problem. Make one law change free (no decree) on the turn the nation advances an age, with a prompt chip ("New age: one free law change"). **Why:** Civ VI's free swap on civic completion sets a rhythm of small, regular choices, which is what our mid-game lacks. **How:** a `freeLawChange` flag set by the age-advance step and spent by the law action in the reducer before it charges a decree; the chip comes from `ui/promptStack.js`. Priority: medium (cheap).

### Diplomacy: weigh war weariness by where the war is fought

**Civ rule.** Civ VI adds war weariness from every battle against a major civ, doubled outside your borders, and turns each 400 points into -1 amenity per turn (**verified** as a wiki extract: [War weariness (Civ6)](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/War_weariness_(Civ6))). Gathering Storm's grievances charge for declarations and conquests and decay over time (**verified**: [Grievances (Civ6)](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Grievances_(Civ6))); the exact table is **unverified**. Civ VII locks suzerainty over an Independent Power for the Age once you win it (**verified** as a wiki extract: [Suzerain (Civ7)](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Suzerain_(Civ7))). Civ VI's top difficulty gives the AI flat yield bonuses (about +40%) and extra start units (**verified** as guide summaries: [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/civilization-6-difficulty-levels-explained/)).

**Our rule.** War exhaustion: per turn at war `2 + min(3, own cities besieged) + 10 x share of own cities held by the enemy`, plus battles (loser `2 + 12 x loss share`, winner 1), times a law multiplier, cap 100 (`src/engine/warBurden.js`, `src/engine/aftermath.js:41-44`). Opinion is an itemised sum; AI war declarations are a flat 2% roll for Tier-1 nations scaled by factors, with an expected-value target pick (`src/utils/aiLogic.js:80-92`, `src/engine/warOdds.js`). Independents join a major by held attitude or overwhelming strength (`src/engine/indepPolicy.js`).

**Gap and change.** Our exhaustion already tracks real costs, but a battle deep in enemy land weighs the same as one at home. Multiply the battle term by 1.5 for the side fighting on a tile it does not own (to tune; Civ's x2 applies to a per-battle points system and is a direction, not a constant to copy). **Why:** it makes defensive wars cheaper and long offensives costlier, which supports the "quiet world" design. **How:** in `aftermath.js`, read the battle tile's owner from `state.world.tileOwner` and scale that side's battle increment. The flat 2% war roll, the fixed battle bars and opinion decay are rows 2, 5, 6 and 7 of `plans/research-ai-math.md`; leave them parked until the owner restarts that plan. Keep difficulty as behaviour (think rate, retreat share, economy targets) rather than Civ's flat bonuses, which players call cheating (an inference from forum complaints in the notes, **unverified**). Priority: medium.

### Victory and length: 923 turns needs an early end for runaway games

**Civ rule.** Civ VI's Standard speed is 500 turns and Online 250 (**verified**: [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/civilization-vi/civ-6-difficulties-speeds-map-types-options)). Civ VII's 1.4.0 patch added Dominance victories: a lead over second place held 5 turns ends the game, with the required lead falling from 6x early to 1.25x at the end (**verified**: [1.4.0 notes](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/game-update-notes/2026-may-19-patch-1-4-0/)). Polytopia's 30-turn Perfection mode is the proven short phone session (**verified**: [Wikipedia, Polytopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battle_of_Polytopia)).

**Our rule.** The game ends at 440 (Fast), 923 (Normal) or 1514 (Marathon) turns or when a nation finishes the Future age's techs, which cannot fire until that age exists; domination needs the capitals of 25% of every other nation; otherwise majors rank by legacy score (`src/data/ages.js:137-140`, `src/engine/ending.js`, `src/data/victoryConditions.js`).

**Gap.** Normal is almost twice Civ VI's Standard, playtest 4 found a hundred near-empty turns in the middle, and once one nation runs away the rest of the game is a formality. Civ VII's falling threshold ends runaway games without shortening close ones.

**Change.** Add a Dominance end: when the leader's legacy score is at least a set multiple of the second major's for 5 reference turns, the game ends with that leader first. Multiples by share of the turn limit: none before 40%, then 6x, 4x from 55%, 3x from 70%, 2x from 80%, 1.5x from 90% (to tune with balance-sim). **Why:** it trims the runaway tail, gives the player a visible race in the late game, and works the same at every speed. **How:** compute it in `src/engine/ending.js` as a new `endingSignal` reason, with the hold time through `speedTurns(5)` (`src/data/ages.js:179-183`) and integer comparisons (`score x 2 >= second x 3` style) so it stays deterministic; a prompt chip warns when any major passes the current multiple. A 120-turn short scenario for phones, in the Polytopia mould, is a separate low-priority option. Priority: high.

### Phone play: radial commands and control groups, not auto-battles

**AoE and Civ evidence.** AoE2 DE on Xbox moves every command into a context radial (hold RT), puts control groups on a second radial, and selects an idle villager with one button; reviewers praised the result (**verified**: [ageofempires.com, Xbox](https://www.ageofempires.com/learn-to-play/match-goals-advanced-controls-xbox/); [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2023/01/30/age-of-empires-ii-xbox-edition-hands-on-great-gamepad-controls/)). AoE Mobile's mostly automatic, hero-led battles were called bland (**verified**: [But Why Tho](https://butwhytho.net/2024/10/age-of-empires-mobile-tacks-light-combat-onto-click-and-wait-gameplay/)). Civ VI on iPhone was praised for touch control, with small text and wrong-tile taps as its main faults (**verified**: [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/231955/civilization-vi-iphone-review.html)).

**Our rule.** Battle input is tap, one-finger pan, drag a formation line from a selected squad, double-tap-drag lasso, long-press for context (attack-move on ground), pinch zoom, and Select and Path modes; squad pick circles are at least 22 px with 1.45x reach for enemies (`src/battle/input/gestures.js`, `src/battle/input/squadPick.js`). The HUD has an idle-worker button. A search of `src/components/battle/` and `src/battle/` found no control groups, and the order schema has no queued orders beyond `movePath`.

**Change.** Two additions. Control groups: four slots on the right rail ("hold to save the selection, tap to select"), which is pure UI over existing selection, with no order schema change and no replay impact. A radial on long-press over a selection with the five commands that matter (attack-move, hold, retreat, ability, formation line), replacing hunting for HUD buttons at 844x390. Add an "Idle squads n" chip next to the idle-worker one, the battle twin of the map's Idle chip, since idle squads were playtest 4's worst battle complaint (P4-H4). **Why:** the console route (same sim, compressed input) is the one with praised reviews; the mobile auto route is the one with poor ones. **How:** groups live in `TacticalBattleScreen.jsx` state; the radial reuses the long-press path in `gestures.js` and must register with `useInteraction` so it obeys the one-panel rule; check both with `node scripts/ui/panels-shots.mjs` and battle-lab's browser screenshot at 844x390. Priority: high.

Several rules from both series should stay out. AoE's armour subtraction would cut the shared multiplier that keeps Command and Auto in step. AoE's monk conversion is a capture, which decision 37 forbids. AoE's and Civ's lack of morale is closer to the owner's rule than Total War's routing, and our asymmetric Shaken state already honours it, so no player-side rout should ever be added. Civ VII's hard age reset and forced civ swap are the clearest case in the evidence of a rule players rejected, and the Continuity and single-civ patches show where Firaxis had to go back to: our design starts there.

## Conclusion

The comparison changes the question from "which rules should we borrow" to "where does our own linkage leak". Terra Imperium's distinctive bet is that a real-time battle and a turn-based empire can share one ledger, one multiplier and one outcome service; the riskiest rules are therefore the places where the two halves quietly disagree (the morale break point), where one half's pace swamps the other (30-minute assaults inside a 923-turn game), and where the phone asks one thumb to run an army, a base and an empire at once. Fixing those three costs less code than any single new system, and every fix keeps the engine seeded and the battle sim integer.

The second takeaway is about timing. The eight-ages plan is about to add Information and Future, and two of today's rules (the `baseAttack` ratio and the research cost tail) would force invented numbers for those ages. Replacing them with a fixed age-step factor and a refitted cost ladder first means the new ages arrive as data, not as a new round of balancing, which is the opposite of how Civ VII had to spend fifteen months of patches walking back its age transitions.

Not checked here: whether P4-C2, P4-H2 and P4-H6 are fixed, how our units regain strength between battles, and the AoE and Civ numbers marked unverified above; every proposed constant needs a balance-sim or battle-lab run before it ships.
