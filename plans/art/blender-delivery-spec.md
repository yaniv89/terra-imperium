# Terra Imperium: delivering the art as Blender models (for GPT)

This file says **how** to deliver each item as a Blender model instead of 2D sheets. It does not
change **what** to make: the item list, the ids, the folders, the regions, the ages, the
variants and the descriptions are exactly those of `plans/art-image-spec.md` (sections 3, 3b, 4,
5 and 6), and its style notes still apply. Where this file and the image spec disagree on how to
deliver, this file wins; on what to make, the image spec wins.

The finished files are brought into the game by `scripts/blender/import_model.py` and then
checked by `scripts/blender/validate_model.py`. Deliver what the rules below ask and an item goes
into the game in seconds; break them and it waits for hand fixes.

---

## 1. Rules for every file

### 1.1 Format
- One `.blend` (editable) **and** one `.glb` (exported) per item, same base name.
- Blender 4.2 or later. No cameras, lights, armatures, modifiers left unapplied, or hidden
  objects in the exported GLB.
- Keep your `build.py` beside it if the model is generated by script, so it can be rebuilt.

### 1.2 Units, scale, direction
- **1 Blender unit = 10 metres.** A 12 m house is 1.2 units wide.
- Model at **real widths and depths**; multiply **heights of buildings by 1.3** (a 3.2 m storey
  is 0.416 units). Landmarks and palaces: use the heights in section 3 below, not 1.3x.
- **Z is up. The front faces Blender -Y** (south: the side the game camera sees). The glTF
  exporter turns this into +Z; leave its default "+Y up" on.
- **Origin at the centre of the footprint, at ground level.** Nothing below Z = 0.

### 1.3 Objects and names
- **Every separate piece is its own object**, named exactly as section 3 says (`house-poor`,
  `house-common`, `house-rich`, `landmark`, `palace`...). Never join different houses into one
  mesh: the game places each house type on its own spot in the town layouts.
- Each object's origin at its own footprint centre, ground level, front to -Y.
- **No ground plate under buildings.** The game lays its own blended ground; a plate under a
  house shows as a hard-edged square. Exceptions: fields (section 3.6) and the camp (3.5),
  whose ground is part of the item.

### 1.4 Materials (only these names)
| Material | Use |
|---|---|
| `Town` | everything built: walls, roofs, wood, stone, metal, glass, plants |
| `Team` | the nation's colour: flags, banners, awnings, painted trims. Authored **neutral light grey `#BFBFBF`** with greyscale folds; the game tints it. 5 to 15% of the surface, never more |
| `Ground` | only for the ground of fields and the camp |
Glass is part of `Town` (an opaque dark-blue tint in the texture): the game has no transparent
building glass. Do not use other material names; extra materials are merged into `Town`.

### 1.5 Textures
- **One texture set per file**: base colour (sRGB) and, if you can, normal and roughness. Up to
  2048 x 2048. All objects in the file share it (one atlas). PNG or WebP, packed in the GLB.
- **Bake ambient occlusion into the base colour** (darker under eaves, in corners, at the foot
  of walls). This is the single biggest thing between a flat toy look and the game's look.
- Paint real material detail: brick courses, roof tiles, plaster wear, wood grain, window
  frames. Windows and doors may be painted on the walls (cheaper than modelled ones).
- No text, letters or real national emblems anywhere.
- UVs must not overlap between surfaces that should look different.

### 1.6 Triangle budgets (LOD0, the model you make)
| Item | Triangles |
|---|---|
| a house type | 2,500 |
| a landmark, a palace, the camp | 15,000 |
| a wall ring | 12,000 |
| a field | 8,000 |
| a wonder tier | 60,000 |
| a tile improvement | 8,000 |
The importer makes the two lighter levels (LOD1, LOD2) itself; you do not need to.
Keep big flat faces simple and spend triangles where the silhouette shows: roofs, towers,
domes, battlements, chimneys.

## 2. Folders and file names

Same folders and ids as the image spec, with `model.glb` and `model.blend` in place of the PNG
views, plus one `preview.png` (the three-quarter view from the front left, 45 degrees up):

```
plans/art/kits/<style>/<age>/houses/model.glb        house-poor, house-common, house-rich (+ props)
plans/art/kits/<style>/<age>/landmark-1/model.glb    landmark
plans/art/kits/<style>/<age>/landmark-2/model.glb    landmark
plans/art/kits/<style>/kingdoms/palace-small/model.glb
plans/art/kits/<style>/kingdoms/palace/model.glb
plans/art/kits/<style>/kingdoms/walls-medium/model.glb
plans/art/towns/<age>/<id>/model.glb                 section 3 items (palaces, walls, camp, fields)
plans/art/buildings/<id>/model.glb                    section 4
plans/art/wonders/<id>/model.glb                      section 5 (tier1, tier2, tier3 objects)
plans/art/improvements/<age>/<id>/model.glb           section 6
```
The sub-landmarks the image batches added stay as they are (`landmark-japan`, `landmark-korea`,
`landmark-east`, `landmark-north`, `landmark-colonies`): one `landmark` object each.

## 3. What each item holds

### 3.1 Kit `houses` (image spec 3b.3 `houses.png`)
One file with three objects, the region and age's three house types:
| Object | Footprint (W x D) | Storeys | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `house-poor` | 7 to 9 m x 6 to 8 m | 1 | the plainest roof and wall of the region |
| `house-common` | 9 to 12 m x 8 to 10 m | 1 to 2 | the region's typical house |
| `house-rich` | 12 to 16 m x 10 to 12 m | 2 | courtyard, porch, garden wall if the region has one; still inside the footprint |
Optional objects in the same file for street props, each its own object at real size:
`prop-well`, `prop-shrine`, `prop-stall` (with a `Team` awning), `prop-tree`, `prop-jars`,
`prop-cart`. Two to six props is plenty.

### 3.2 Kit `street` and `roofscape` (3b.3)
Not separate models. Deliver `street` as the **houses file laid out**: the 3 by 3 block as nine
**linked duplicates** (Alt+D) of the three house objects on the street surface, saved as
`street/model.blend` with a `preview.png` from the game camera; the importer reads the spacing
from it. `roofscape` is only `roofscape/preview.png`, the same block rendered straight down.
The street surface itself (paving, cobbles, packed earth, asphalt) is a texture swatch in
`materials/` (below); do not model the street as a plate.

### 3.3 Kit `landmark-1`, `landmark-2` and sub-landmarks (3b.3, 3b.4)
One object `landmark` per file. Size: **12 to 25 m across, never more than 30 m**, and the
height the image spec gives for that age's town (the tallest landmark: 16 m small, 24 m
medium, 36 m big, modern 50 m; a landmark may be up to that). A great monument is shown as its
signature part at this size (a gopuram gate, not the whole temple compound). Flags or banners
on it as `Team`.

### 3.4 Kit `materials` (3b.3)
`materials/swatches.png`: square tileable swatches, 512 px each, labelled: wall, roof, street
surface, wood, trim, team cloth (grey). The street-surface swatch must tile seamlessly.

### 3.5 Section 3 town items
| Id | Object(s) | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `palace-small` | `palace` | 8 m across, about 8 m high | stands in the town's free centre; bigger does not fit |
| `palace` | `palace` | 12 m across, 12 to 16 m to the top of a flag | the same; one strong silhouette |
| `walls-small` | `walls` | outer diameter 44 m | a ring round the 40 m town, gate at the south (-Y), 3 to 4 m high (x1.3) |
| `walls-medium` | `walls` | 64 to 66 m | round the 60 m town, 4 to 6 m high (x1.3) |
| `walls-big` | `walls` | 86 to 90 m | round the 80 m town, 6 to 8 m high (x1.3) |
| `colony-camp` | `camp` | 18 x 16 m | with its own ground patch (`Ground`), irregular edge |
| `field-1` .. `field-4` | `field` | 10 to 16 m long | with its own ground (`Ground`), almost flat, an irregular edge |
| `town-small` / `-medium` / `-big` (base towns) | see 3.7 | | |
Walls: the gate faces -Y; leave the inside empty (the town stands there). Build the ring as
connected arcs, with towers and the gate as their own objects in the same file
(`walls`, `tower`, `gate`), so the game can repeat a tower round the ring.

### 3.6 Fields
Seen from far above: the pattern (rows, trees, beds, water) must read from straight above.
Plants as a few large shapes, not thousands of small ones.

### 3.7 Base towns (`town-small`, `town-medium`, `town-big`, variants a and b)
Deliver them as **kits**, not whole towns: the houses file (3.1) and the two landmarks (3.3) of
that age's base style, plus a `layout.blend` that places linked duplicates of them on the town
footprint (40, 60 or 80 m, the centre 12 m left free for the palace), the way 3.2 lays out the
street. The game already has layouts for every base town; a `layout.blend` is only needed if
you want a different one.

### 3.8 Buildings, wonders, improvements (sections 4, 5, 6)
- Building landmarks (section 4): one object `landmark`, rules of 3.3.
- Wonders (section 5): one file with three objects `tier1`, `tier2`, `tier3` at the same
  origin, up to 120 m across (12 units), 60,000 triangles each.
- Tile improvements (section 6): one object `improvement` on a 50 x 50 m patch (5 x 5 units)
  with its own `Ground`, 8,000 triangles.

---

## 4. Check before you hand over

1. In Blender, every object's front faces -Y, its origin is at its footprint centre at Z = 0,
   and its dimensions match the tables (N panel: Dimensions, in units of 10 m).
2. Only `Town`, `Team` and `Ground` materials. Team parts are grey.
3. Triangle count (Statistics overlay) within budget.
4. No ground plate under buildings; no hidden objects, cameras or lights.
5. Export GLB: Format glTF Binary, Include: Selected Objects off (export all), Transform +Y up,
   Mesh: Apply Modifiers on, Materials: Export, Images: Automatic or WebP.
6. `preview.png` beside it.

If your model is generated by a Python script, the `validate_model.py` check can be run on the
GLB: `python3 scripts/blender/validate_model.py model.glb out landmark 2.0 1.6`.
